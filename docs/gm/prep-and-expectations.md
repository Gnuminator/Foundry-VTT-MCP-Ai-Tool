# Prep and expectations

Two questions every new GM asks: "what should I prepare?" and "how do we make sure we all want
the same game?". This page gives a short answer to both, plus an agenda for session 0 that you can
use, change or ignore. Session 0 is yours to plan: nothing here is a rule.

Your [GM coach](gm-coach.md) has this page. Ask it to help you plan, and it asks you a few
questions first, suggests, and leaves the choices to you. It works on the free Claude plan.

## What to prepare for a session

Prepare what the players are likely to meet next, not a script. Players rarely do what you expect,
so a list of pieces you can use in any order goes further than a planned evening. About 30 to 60
minutes is enough for most sessions.

1. **Where you left off.** Read the last session's notes and decide how the night opens: the first
   scene, ready to describe.
2. **Three to five scenes that might happen.** One line each: where, who, what is going on. Some
   won't happen; keep them for later.
3. **The people they may meet.** For each: a name, what they want, one thing that makes them easy
   to remember (a voice, a habit, a look).
4. **Places.** The next one or two locations, with the scene ready in Foundry (map, walls, lights).
5. **Things they can find out.** Clues, rumours and secrets, written as single facts. Hand each out
   where it fits, not only where the book puts it.
6. **Fights that may happen.** The monsters, their stat blocks open once in Foundry, and the tokens
   ready. A quick look at their attacks and special moves saves time at the table.
7. **Rewards.** Treasure, items or information the players can earn tonight.

You can skip: writing out dialogue, planning what the players will do, and rules you can look up
when they come up ("I'll rule it this way now and check after the session" is fine).

On the Foundry side, [Before each session](before-session.md) is the 15-minute check before play.

## Talking expectations through

Most problems at a table come from people wanting different games, not from rules. A short talk
before the campaign starts saves a lot. Pick the topics that matter to your group:

- **What everyone enjoys most:** fights, talking to characters, exploring, puzzles, a strong story.
  The campaign will have all of them; this tells you where to spend time.
- **Tone:** how dark or scary the game may get. Agree on **lines** (things that never happen in
  the game) and **veils** (things that happen, but off screen). Anyone may say "let's skip this"
  at any time, no questions asked.
- **Character death:** can it happen, and what then? (See [When a character dies](death-and-new-characters.md).)
- **One party:** the characters travel together and have a reason to. No fights between players'
  characters unless everyone agrees.
- **Rules:** the 2024 Player's Handbook. The GM makes a ruling at the table and looks it up after.
  Knowing what your own character can do is each player's job.
- **Schedule:** how often you play, how long, and what happens to a character when its player is
  away.
- **Table habits:** cameras and mics, breaks, phones, chat in Discord during play.
- **The tool:** what Claude does for the GM, and the recording (each player answers the recording
  message before anything is recorded; see the [player guide](../player/README.md)).
- **What the group wants from the campaign:** play it to the end, roughly how long, how much the
  story matters compared with the fights.

Write down what you agree on and post it in Discord, so nobody has to remember it.

## The ability score talk

Everyone at the table uses the same method. The 2024 Player's Handbook offers three:

| Method             | How it works                                                      | Good for                                           |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Standard array** | Everyone gets the same six numbers: 15, 14, 13, 12, 10, 8.        | Fair and quick. No character is much stronger.     |
| **Point buy**      | Each player spends 27 points on their scores (each from 8 to 15). | More choice, still fair.                           |
| **Rolling**        | Roll four six-sided dice per score and drop the lowest.           | Surprises: some characters very strong, some weak. |

Ask the players what they think, then decide. Set the method in Actor Studio before anyone opens
the builder ([Prepare session 0](prepare-session-0.md), step 3). If you pick rolling, decide too
whether everyone rolls where the others can see it.

## A session 0 agenda you can change

Session 0 here is online: Discord voice, Foundry in the browser. Times are rough; drop, move or
shorten anything.

| #   | What                                                                                                                  | About   |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| 1   | **Hello and tech check.** Everyone is in Discord voice and logged in to Foundry ([Join the game](../player/join.md)). | 15 min  |
| 2   | **The pitch.** What kind of campaign this is, in a few sentences, without spoilers.                                   | 10 min  |
| 3   | **Expectations talk.** The topics above that matter to you.                                                           | 30 min  |
| 4   | **Ability scores and options.** The talk above; then set the method in Actor Studio while the players wait.           | 10 min  |
| 5   | **Characters.** Players build ([Make your character](../player/make-a-character.md)); you check each sheet.           | 60 min  |
| 6   | **How the characters know each other.** One link per character to another, or a reason they travel together.          | 15 min  |
| 7   | **An intro scene or prologue (optional).** Easily half a session. If time runs short, it opens session 1 instead.     | 60+ min |
| 8   | **Wrap up.** The next date, and what players should read before then.                                                 | 5 min   |

Without the intro scene this is about 2.5 hours; with it, 3.5 to 4. Breaks every hour or so help
online.

A message to post in Discord a few days before (change the words to your own):

> Session 0 is on [date] at [time], online. Please have Foundry open and logged in before we start.
> We'll talk about what kind of game we want, pick how we get ability scores, and make our
> characters together. Think about a class you'd like to play, and bring any questions or wishes
> for the campaign.

## Ask your coach

Start a new chat for each topic; long chats use up the free plan's limit faster
([Claude Free or Pro](claude-plans.md)). You can ask in Danish.

- "GM coach: help me plan session 0. We are four players, online, about three hours. Ask me
  questions first, then suggest an agenda."
- "GM coach: write the questions for our expectations talk, short, so I can post them in Discord."
- "GM coach: compare the standard array, point buy and rolling for a group of new players."
- "GM coach: here are my notes for the next session. What should I prepare, and what can I skip?"
  (Paste your own notes; the coach has no adventure text of its own.)
- "GM coach: after session 0 we agreed on this. Turn it into a short list for Discord."
